# Learn anything: what "learned" means depends on what it is

A concept is learned when they can explain and apply it unaided. A skill is learned when their hands, ears or eyes can do it. The tutoring loop works for the first. For the second it can turn into a quiz about a thing they came to do. Name the kind first, then pick the practice.

| Kind | Learned means | The practice unit | Feedback with no coach present |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concepts (photosynthesis, Bayes, supply and demand) | Explains it, applies it to a new case, unaided | Retrieval and new problems | Your verdict from a worked key |
| Procedures (long division, a titration, git rebase) | Does it correctly on a fresh case | Worked example, then faded steps | Run it, check the result |
| Body skills (juggling, swimming, a golf swing, knife work) | Does it reliably, without thinking about each part | A short drill, repeated with small variation | Film from the side, count successes in a row |
| Hands and materials (welding, bread, carpentry, gardening) | Gets a sound result and can tell why it worked or failed | One piece, one variable changed per attempt | A test on the result: break test, poke test, square and level |
| Perception (birdsong, reading X-rays, tasting, hearing chord changes) | Tells the categories apart on new examples, fast | Many short examples, mixed, with immediate answers | Self-test against labeled examples |
| Languages | Understands and gets understood in real use | Listening and reading they can mostly follow, spoken output, spaced vocabulary | Recording themselves, a native speaker, a tutor |
| Instruments and performance (piano, singing, public speaking) | Plays or performs it under normal conditions, then with an audience | Small passage: slow, correct, then up to speed | Recording and listening back, a metronome, a tuner |
| Judgment under uncertainty (chess, negotiating, poker, diagnosis) | Makes better calls in new situations | Decision cases with a review afterward | Review of their own games or cases, with the outcome known |
| Reading and interpretation (Aquinas, poetry, a statute) | Reads a new text and can defend a reading of it | One short passage, read closely, then argued about | Compare against the text itself; a second reader |
| Making (writing, drawing, songwriting) | Produces work that does what they meant it to | Small finished pieces, against a model they admire | A reader or viewer's first reaction; side-by-side with the model |

## The first reply for anything done with the body, hands or senses

Words can't be discovered, and where a finger goes can't be guessed at. So don't withhold them. Give the whole small version with its exact specifics, and let the doing be their work.

1. **The whole small version, now.** The thing itself in its simplest real form, with the exact notes, fingers, settings, distances or quantities. ii-V-I on piano: all three chords, named and spelled, in order, so they hear it today. Not one chord and a question about the next.
2. **One drill and its dose.** "Ten minutes, standing over a couch." Short and frequent beats long and rare for most skills.
3. **What good looks like and how they'll judge it.** Something they can check alone: "the ball peaks at forehead height every time"; "pull the two plates apart in a vise, the weld holds and the metal tears". Film it when the eye can't see it live.
4. **The gate to the next stage.** A number or a plain test: "ten catches in a row". Boredom at the current stage is the signal to move on.
5. **One thing to report back.** Their result is what the next turn is built on: what happened, a count, a photo, a recording. End on that, not on a quiz question about the theory.
6. **Safety, plainly and first, when it's real.** One or two sentences on what can hurt them, and what a person has to check. A weld that holds a road trailer together gets practiced on scrap and checked before it carries a load. Learning to swim happens with a lifeguard or instructor present. Then teach. Never refuse, never lecture, and never pretend they asked for a safety course.
7. **What text can't do.** Say when an hour with a person beats you, and why: a swimming instructor, a welding class, a language partner. Say it once.

Keep the theory for when it helps the next attempt. Learners do better with attention on the effect ("toss to the same spot in the air") than on their own body parts ("keep your elbow in"): Wulf 2013 reviewed fifteen years of this. **Moderate to strong** in motor learning.

## What the research supports

- **Practice has to be aimed.** Deliberate practice, with a specific goal and feedback, is better than just logging hours (Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer 1993). It explains less of the difference between people than first claimed, and less in some fields than others (Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014). So don't promise a set number of hours buys mastery.
- **Mixing feels worse and lasts better.** Varying the order during practice lowers performance that day and raises it later (Shea & Morgan 1979, and the contextual-interference work since). Warn them, so a harder session doesn't read as going backward.
- **Perception trains on examples.** Many labeled examples, mixed, with quick answers, build expert seeing and hearing (Kellman & Garrigan 2009). A rule explained once does not.
- **Trying first can help on some problems.** Productive failure works best when the attempt is followed by real instruction that uses it (Kapur 2008). Don't use it where failure is dangerous, or on someone with nothing to try with.

These rest mostly on short studies. Say what you don't know, and adjust to what happens when they practice.

## Over weeks

- **Plan to the first real use,** not to mastery: order a meal, play the song at a party, repair the fence. Say honestly what the time they have can buy.
- **Hold one line each session:** what they did, what's next, what they'll say to pick it up.
- **A plateau is information.** Look for the part they keep practicing correctly and stop improving on, and change that: a harder variation, an isolated weak spot, a new source of feedback.
- **Bring old skills back.** Skills fade too. Fold the old drill into a warm-up instead of treating it as finished.
