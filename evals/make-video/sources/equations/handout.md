# MATH 0960 handout: Solving two-step equations (synthetic eval material)

Section 2.3, page 1.

To solve an equation means to find the value of the variable that makes the equation true.

Inverse operations undo each other. Addition and subtraction are inverse operations. Multiplication and division are inverse operations.

Whatever you do to one side of an equation, you must do to the other side.

To solve a two-step equation, first undo the addition or subtraction, then undo the multiplication or division.

Example: Solve 2x + 3 = 11. Subtract 3 from both sides: 2x = 8. Divide both sides by 2: x = 4.

Always check your answer by substituting it back into the original equation. Check: 2(4) + 3 = 11, and 11 = 11.

Common mistake: dividing only one term by the coefficient. In 2x + 3 = 11, dividing by 2 first means you must divide every term, including the 3.
